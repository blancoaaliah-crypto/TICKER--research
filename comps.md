Lab 07 — Comparable-Company Policy and Implied Range
Define / Discover

P/E measures how much investors are paying for each $1 of a company’s earnings. It is calculated as share price divided by diluted EPS. P/E helps compare companies of different sizes and provides a market-based check against a DCF valuation.

A lower P/E does not automatically mean a better investment because it may reflect slower growth, higher risk, weaker profitability, or other business concerns.

Peer Policy

I evaluated AutoNation and Group 1 as peers for Asbury based on their business models. All three operate large franchised automotive dealership businesses with vehicle sales and service/parts operations.

The companies are not identical, so differences in growth, profitability, business mix, and risk should still be considered when interpreting the comparison.

Leave-One-Out Analysis

Removing Group 1 leaves AutoNation as the only peer. The implied Asbury price becomes $215.81, which is $15.18 lower than the full-peer median estimate.

Because only one peer remains, there is no longer a valuation range. The result is only a reference estimate based on that single peer.

Interpretation

The comparable-company valuation does not prove that Asbury is fairly valued. P/E multiples can differ because of growth expectations, risk, profitability, and accounting differences. The peer comparison should be used as another valuation perspective alongside the DCF.
